# Full Mock Exam: CISSP Simulation Guide

CISSP uses Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT) — 100–150 questions in 3 hours, with no ability to skip or go back. This guide is for running your own timed, full-length practice sessions to simulate real exam conditions. **Do this in Week 8, before booking your exam** — see `study-plan.md`.

---

## When You're Ready for a Full Mock

- [ ] All 8 domain knowledge checks on the Practice sheet completed with 9/10 or better
- [ ] `resources/exam-traps.md` reviewed and understood
- [ ] `resources/mindset-guide.md` read and internalized
- [ ] `resources/quick-reference.md` memorized (can recall it without looking)
- [ ] A substantial bank of practice questions completed beyond this workspace's 80 — see platforms below

If you haven't hit these benchmarks, continue domain study first.

## Recommended Mock Exam Platforms

This workspace's 80 domain knowledge-check questions are for domain recall, not exam simulation — they're too few, and too direct, to substitute for a full-length adaptive-style mock. For that, use a dedicated question bank:

| Platform | Approx. question bank | Why use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Boson ExSim-Max** | 750+ | Widely regarded as the hardest and most realistic simulation of the real exam's difficulty |
| **Wiley Efficient Learning / Sybex practice tests** | 1,000+ | Paired with the official Sybex CISSP study guide |
| **CCCure quiz engine** | 2,000+ | Large question bank, good for domain-specific drilling |
| **ISC2's own practice tests / official app** | Varies | From the exam maker — use as a supplement, not a sole source |

> A common rule of thumb in the CISSP community: if you're consistently scoring 75%+ on Boson ExSim-Max, you're ready — it's generally considered harder than the live exam.

## How to Run a Timed Mock Session

**Setup**
1. Find 3 uninterrupted hours — no phone, no other tabs, no pausing.
2. Use a platform that presents ~150 questions in sequence with no going back, to mirror CAT conditions as closely as a non-adaptive tool can.
3. Set a timer for 3 hours and do not look at notes or reference material.

**During**
- Aim for roughly 72 seconds per question on average (3 hours ÷ 150 questions), but don't watch the clock obsessively — CAT can end anywhere from 100–150 questions.
- If stuck, eliminate the obviously wrong answers and commit — you cannot return to a question on the real CAT exam.
- Apply the Manager Filter from `resources/mindset-guide.md` on every question.
- Watch for keywords: *first, best, most, primary, immediate.*

**After**
- Score your results by domain.
- For every wrong answer, identify why the correct answer is correct **and** why each wrong answer is wrong — this is where most of the learning happens.
- Log your domain scores below.

## Readiness Benchmarks

| Score | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Below 65% | Not ready | Continue domain study, focused on the lowest-scoring domains |
| 65–74% | Getting close | More practice questions; revisit `resources/exam-traps.md`; re-run the weak domain checks |
| 75–79% | Close to ready | One more mock; confirm no domain is below 60% |
| 80%+ on a hard bank (e.g. Boson) | Ready | Book your exam |

The real CISSP pass mark is 700/1000 — a scaled score, not a raw percentage, and item difficulty is weighted in CAT. Consistently scoring 75%+ on a hard third-party bank is a reasonable proxy for readiness.

## Exam Day Checklist

**Night before**
- [ ] Review `resources/exam-traps.md` one final time
- [ ] Review `resources/quick-reference.md`, especially SLE/ALE/ARO
- [ ] Confirm your Pearson VUE appointment and testing center address, or online proctoring setup
- [ ] Set two alarms; get 7–8 hours of sleep

**Morning of**
- [ ] Light breakfast — something you tolerate well
- [ ] No cramming — trust your preparation
- [ ] Arrive at the testing center 30 minutes early (or log in early for online proctoring)
- [ ] Bring the required forms of government-issued ID matching your exam registration exactly
