00Beginner Start

Prepare to think like a security leader — across all 8 domains, from zero.

CISSP is broad, not deep: it certifies that you can reason about security governance, architecture, and operations at a management level across the entire CBK (Common Body of Knowledge), not that you're a hands-on specialist in any one domain. Core rule: study the reasoning, not just the vocabulary — the exam rewards the "best" answer, not merely a technically correct one.

02Study Method

KNOW → RECOGNIZE → APPLY → DEFEND

Use the same mental loop for every topic across all 8 domains.

❓ What is it?

Learn the concept, model, or control precisely — including what it does not do.

🔍 What is this scenario really testing?

A question rarely names the concept directly — spot the domain and the specific model/control it's pointing at.

⚡ What's the best answer?

Several options may be technically true. Pick the one a risk-aware security leader would choose given the full context.

💡 Why is it best?

Be able to explain why the runner-up answer is wrong, not just why your answer is right.

Example

"This scenario describes [situation]. That points to [domain/concept]. Of the plausible options, [answer] is most appropriate because [reason] — whereas [runner-up] would also address part of the problem but [why it's second-best]."

Why "best answer," not "only correct answer"

CISSP is written from a generalist security-management perspective. A deeply technical, single-vendor-specific fix is often valid but rarely the most appropriate choice on an exam built around governance, risk, and proportionate response. When two answers both look right, ask: "Which one would the CISO actually choose, given cost, risk, and business context?"

Watch for absolutes

Words like "always," "never," and "only" are frequent — though not universal — signals of an incorrect option in a domain built entirely around trade-offs and context-dependent decisions.

03Domain 1 · Weight 16%

Security and Risk Management

The largest domain — its concepts (risk, governance, ethics) thread through all seven others.

CIA Triad and Its Opposite

PropertyDefinitionOpposite (DAD)
ConfidentialityDisclosed only to authorized subjectsDisclosure
IntegrityAccurate, complete, unmodifiedAlteration
AvailabilityTimely, reliable accessDestruction

Risk Formulas

SLE = AV × EF (Single Loss Expectancy = Asset Value × Exposure Factor)
ALE = SLE × ARO (Annualized Loss Expectancy = SLE × Annualized Rate of Occurrence)

Tip

Four risk treatments: Mitigate, Avoid, Transfer, Accept. Buying insurance is transfer, not mitigation — it changes who pays, not the likelihood of the event.

Governance Hierarchy

Policy (mandatory, high-level) → Standard (mandatory, specific) → Procedure (mandatory, step-by-step) → Guideline (discretionary). A Baseline is a minimum reference configuration state, distinct from a Standard's rule.

Data Roles

RoleResponsibility
Data OwnerAccountable for classification and protection requirements
Data CustodianDay-to-day technical care per the owner's requirements
Data Controller (GDPR)Determines purpose and means of processing

Sanitization Hierarchy

Clear (software overwrite) → Purge (degauss/crypto-erase, more intensive) → Destroy (physical, irreversible — the only acceptable method for the highest sensitivity levels).

Important

08Domain 6 · Weight 12%

Security Assessment and Testing

Vulnerability assessment vs. penetration testing, code-level testing, and audits.

Assessment vs. Testing vs. Audit

A vulnerability assessment finds and lists weaknesses. A penetration test proves they're exploitable and shows real impact — and always requires signed Rules of Engagement first. An audit is an independent, formal check against a standard or policy.

SAST vs. DAST

SAST analyzes source code without running it (earlier, cheaper to fix). DAST tests the running application from outside (catches runtime/config issues SAST can't see). Neither replaces the other.

Tip

SOC 2 Type I = design at a point in time. SOC 2 Type II = design and operating effectiveness over a period — stronger assurance.

09Domain 7 · Weight 13%

Security Operations

Incident response, backups/DR, evidence handling, and change management.

Incident Response Lifecycle

Preparation → Detection & Analysis → Containment → Eradication → Recovery → Lessons Learned. Containment (isolate/stop spread) always comes before eradication (remove root cause).

Backup Trade-off

Incremental: fastest to create, slowest to restore (replay every increment in order). Differential: slower to create over time, faster to restore.

Important

Life safety always outranks asset protection. Fire/emergency exits must fail-safe (unlock) on power loss.

10Domain 8 · Weight 10%

Software Development Security

Secure SDLC, common vulnerability classes, and database security concepts.

Shift Left

Move security activities (threat modeling, secure code review, SAST) earlier in the SDLC — toward requirements/design — because defects found in production cost far more to fix.

Aggregation vs. Inference

Aggregation combines multiple low-sensitivity pieces into a higher-sensitivity picture. Inference deduces a restricted fact from patterns, without ever directly accessing the restricted data.

Tip

The structural fix for SQL injection is parameterized queries — they separate code from data entirely, not just filter bad characters.

At the start

  • ✅ Read every question completely before answering — CAT questions cannot be skipped or revisited
  • ✅ Eliminate obviously wrong answers first, then pick the most complete/appropriate remaining option

  • ✅ Think like a security manager, not a single-tool technician — favor the risk-proportionate answer
  • ✅ Don't try to "pace for 150" — focus on the question in front of you; CAT can end anywhere from 100–150

Domain 1 — Security and Risk Management

Tests 03 Security & Risk Management · full deep-dive →

Domain 1 · Question 1 of 10
A ransomware attack encrypts a hospital's patient records, making them completely inaccessible to staff. Which element of the CIA triad is most directly violated?
✅ Correct answer: B
Why: Encrypted, inaccessible data is primarily an availability failure, even though ransomware often also threatens confidentiality via exfiltration.
Domain 1 · Question 2 of 10
Which of the following best distinguishes a security "standard" from a "baseline"?
✅ Correct answer: B
Why: A standard is a mandatory rule; a baseline is the minimum configuration state used as a reference point.
Domain 1 · Question 3 of 10
An organization purchases cyber insurance to cover the financial impact of a potential data breach. This is an example of which risk treatment strategy?
✅ Correct answer: C
Why: Insurance changes who bears the financial impact — it does not reduce the likelihood of the event, so it is transfer, not mitigation.
Domain 1 · Question 4 of 10
An asset has a value of $200,000. A specific threat is expected to destroy 25% of the asset's value each time it occurs, and is expected to occur twice per year. What is the ALE?
✅ Correct answer: B
Why: SLE = AV × EF = $200,000 × 0.25 = $50,000; ALE = SLE × ARO = $50,000 × 2 = $100,000.
Domain 1 · Question 5 of 10
A company backs up its database nightly at midnight with no other replication. A failure occurs at 6:00 PM. What is the effective RPO exposure in this scenario?
✅ Correct answer: B
Why: With only nightly backups and no replication, up to a full day (up to 18 hours since the last midnight backup) of data could be lost.
Domain 1 · Question 6 of 10
Which threat modeling methodology categorizes threats using Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, and Elevation of Privilege?
✅ Correct answer: C
Why: STRIDE's six categories spell out Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege.
Domain 1 · Question 7 of 10
Which of the four forms of intellectual property protection has no fixed expiration date but loses all protection the moment it is disclosed publicly?
✅ Correct answer: D
Why: Trade secrets last exactly as long as secrecy is maintained — no fixed term, but no protection once disclosed.
Domain 1 · Question 8 of 10
Under the (ISC)² Code of Ethics, when two canons appear to conflict in a given scenario, which canon takes priority?
✅ Correct answer: C
Why: The canons are explicitly ranked; the lower-numbered canon always takes precedence in a conflict.
Domain 1 · Question 9 of 10
A SOC 2 Type II report differs from a SOC 2 Type I report in that it:
✅ Correct answer: B
Why: Type II adds operating effectiveness testing over a review period (commonly 6–12 months), not just a design snapshot.
Domain 1 · Question 10 of 10
Which legal system relies primarily on case law and precedent, and includes criminal, civil/tort, and administrative branches?
✅ Correct answer: B
Why: Common law relies on precedent and has criminal, civil, and administrative branches; civil law systems rely primarily on codified statutes.
